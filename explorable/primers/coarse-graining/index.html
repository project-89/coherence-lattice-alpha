Coarse-graining and renormalizationSquint and the law survives — how physics decides what matters at each scale

Half of this library's boldest sentences lean on one move: zoom out and ask what survives. The scale ladder says each level of nature is the coarse-grained dynamics of the level below. The critical band, where the essays locate minds, is the place where zooming out changes nothing at all. And "one law, every scale" is not poetry — it is a claim about what happens to equations when you squint at them. This module teaches the move itself, from zero, with your hands on the blur knob. It is the last piece of mathematics the essays use that this curriculum had not yet built.

Three sections. You will squint at a lattice and watch noise die while structure survives; you will discover that squinting has destinations — and one impossible temperature that refuses to choose; and you will watch two entirely different machines become indistinguishable the moment you stop looking too closely, which is the whole secret of universality.
