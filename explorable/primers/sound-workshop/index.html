The sound workshopDrawing with phases — two instruments where relationships between rhythms become pictures

Primer 00 taught you to hear phase: beats, detuning, the moment two rhythms lock. This workshop teaches you to see it. Both instruments here are old, real, and beautiful — a Victorian drawing machine and an eighteenth-century sand-covered plate — and both do the same thing: they take a relationship between oscillations, something invisible and abstract, and turn it into a picture you could hang on a wall. By the end, "two phases locking" will not be a definition. It will be a drawing holding still.

This is the primers track's first multi-section workshop: one topic, enough room to do it properly. It assumes only primer 00.

  1. The harmonograph: phase as a drawingA pen driven by two oscillators. Frequency ratios draw the figures your ear calls consonant; detune sets the drawing slowly turning; coupling freezes it — locking, visible.
  2. Chladni plates: sand finds the silenceA vibrating plate, a pinch of sand. Each frequency selects a mode; the sand walks off the moving regions and gathers on the quiet lines — the standing pattern made visible.
  3. Why the pattern: watching vibration flowThe real wave simulation, slowed down: watch ripples propagate and reflect, see resonance build the standing pattern, and probe why whole regions flash in perfect sync.
  4. The Chladni laboratoryThe whole bench: sweep any frequency — past your own hearing — free the edges for Tyndall’s families, press a finger to reselect the figure, couple two drivers and watch the pattern crystallize, and see the sand bounce on a 3D plate.
