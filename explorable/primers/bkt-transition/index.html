The BKT transitionWhy the wall sits at 2/π — the site's central constant finally gets its why

The von Mises module gave the site's central number a birth certificate: R0(KBKT) ≈ 0.303 is the mean resultant of a circular cloud at the critical coupling. But it left the deepest question standing: why is there a critical coupling at all, and why does it sit at exactly K = 2/π — a clean ratio of small integers and π, with no dial anyone tuned? This module is the answer. It is a story about defects: little whirlpools in a field of phases, the energy it costs to have one, the freedom you gain by letting it roam, and the knife-edge where those two logarithms exactly cancel. That knife-edge is the wall the whole library keeps returning to.

This is an advanced module. It assumes phase, winding numbers (you will meet old friends — the loops you drew there come back as whirlpools), and the resultant. Everything else is built here, including the site's missing plain-words account of free energy.

  1. Vortices: whirlpools in a field of phasesA vortex is a point the phase field winds around — a winding number you can place, drag, and fail to comb away. Topological protection, felt.
  2. The argument: energy versus entropyA lone vortex costs πK·ln R; its freedom is worth 2·ln R. Free energy in plain words, both logarithms measured live, and the knife-edge where the slopes cross: K = 2/π.
  3. The wall: what unbinding destroysBelow the wall, free vortices scramble every correlation; above it, bound pairs let order carry. The constant, live to eight decimals, and the hand-off to the essays.
